# Longitudinal and Transverse waves
$$
v = \sqrt{ \frac{E}{d} }
$$
where $E$ is the appropriate modulus and $d$ is the density. For a rod, $E$ is the ***Young's modulus***; for a fluid $E$ is the ***Bulk modulus***.

# Wave Motions

$$
\text{Velocity of wave} = \frac{\text{distance travelled by wave}}{\text{time taken}}
$$
$$
\implies \frac{\text{number of waves} \times \text{ wave length}}{\text{ time}}
$$
$$
velocity = frequency \times wavelength
$$
$$
v = n \lambda
$$
## Relation between Velocity and Temperature

if $v_{t}$ and $v_{o}$ are the velocities of sound at $t\celsius$ and $0\celsius$ respectively,
then 
$$
\frac{v_{t}}{v_{0}} = \sqrt{ \frac{T_{t}}{T_{0}} }
$$
where $T_{t}$ and $T_{0}$ are the absolute temperatures.

>[!danger] Note
>absolute temperature require you to convert to Kelvin scale.


## Intensity of the wave

$$
I = 2 \pi^{2} f^{2} a^{2} vd
$$
where,  $f = frequency$
        $a=amplitude$
        $v=\text{velocty of the wave}$
        $d=\text{density of the medium}$

If $P$ is the power of an isotopic source of sound at a distance $r$, the intensity or loudness of sound.
$$
I = \frac{P}{4\pi r^{2}}
$$
# Mean time to failure 

$$
 MTTF = \frac{\text{ Total operating time of all units}}{\text{ Total number of failures}}
$$
## for repairable systems 
1) MTBF
   $$
MTBF = \dfrac{\text{Total operating time (uptime)}}{\text{Number of Failures}}
$$
2) Failure rate
   Assuming a constant failure rate ( exponential model), 
   $$
\lambda = \frac{1}{MTBF}
$$
3) Availability (steady-state)
   For an exponential repair/arrival model,
   $$
A \approx \frac{MTBF}{MTBF+MTTR}
$$
where $MTTR$ is mean time to repair.
