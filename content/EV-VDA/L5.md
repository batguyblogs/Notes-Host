### EV Component Sizing
Dr.P.Rangarajan


#### Why Proper Sizing Matters?
- Ensures optimal performance, efficiency, and durability
- Matches power delivery to driving demands
- Helps manage energy use and heat generation

#### Main Components to Size:
- Electric Motor - Delivers required torque and speed
- Battery Pack - Stores and supplies energy for driving range and performance
- Power Electronics - Manages Power flow between battery and motor
- Drive train - Transfers motor output to wheels efficiently

#### EV Vs Ice Powertrain Sizing
- **ICE:** Focus on engine displacement and fuel efficiency
- **EV:** Emphasis on motor power, battery capacity and energy efficiency

#### Unique EV Challenges
- Must deliver *instantaneous power* for quick acceleration
- Sustain power for **cruising and uphill driving**
- Enable *regenerative breaking* for energy recovery
- Require **thermal management** to avoid overheating and ensure reliability

## Vehicle Dynamics
Understanding the Forces that move an Electric Vehicle:
**Rolling Resistance:** Opposition to motion due to tire deformation and road surface interaction

*Aerodynamic Drag:* Air resistance acting against the vehicles motion

**Grade Force:** Component of weight acting along the road on an incline

*Inertial Force:* Force required to accelerate the vehicle and its mass

### Step by Step Procedures:
 
#### 1. Rolling Resistance Analysis for Electric Vehicle Applications

##### 1.Understand the Rolling Resistance Formula
The rolling resistance force is calculated using:

$$
F_{rr} = \mu_{r} \times m \times g \times \cos\theta
$$


>[!note]
>For a flat road, $\theta = 0^{\circ}$  
>Since $\cos0^{\circ} = 1$,
>the equation simplifies to $F_{rr} = \mu \times m \times g$

| Symbol    | Description                       | Unit          |
| --------- | --------------------------------- | ------------- |
| $F_{rr}$  | Rolling Resistance Force          | N             |
| $\mu_{r}$ | Rolling Resistance Coefficient    | -             |
| $m$       | Vehicle Mass                      | kg            |
| $g$       | Gravitational Acceleration (9.81) | ${m}/{s^{2}}$ |
| $\theta$  | Road Inclination Angle            | degrees       |
##### 2. Understand the Physical Origin of Rolling Resistance

| Cause                                   | Contribution    |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Tyre deformation<br>(Rubber Hysteresis) | $\approx 90 \%$ |
| Road surface indentation                | $\approx 5 \%$  |
| Wheel Bearing friction                  | $\approx 5 \%$  |
###### 1. Tyre Deformation
- Front portion compresses
- Rear portion expands
- Rubber is not perfectly elastic
- Energy is lost as heat every rotation
- This phenomenon is called hysteresis loss.

###### 2. Road Surface Indentation
- Soft road surface deform under vehicle weight.
- Examples: Gravel, Sand, Mud
- Energy is spent deforming the road.

###### 3. Bearing Friction
- Wheel bearings rotate continuously.
- Mechanical friction inside bearings also consumes energy

##### 3. Select the Rolling Resistance Coefficient ($\mu_{r}$)

| Surface                        | $\mu_{r}$ Value |
| ------------------------------ | --------------- |
| Smooth Asphalt (Passenger Car) | 0.010 - 0.015   |
| EV Low Rolling Resistance Tyre | 0.007 - 0.012   |
| Gravel                         | 0.020 - 0.035   |
| Sand                           | 0.050 - 0.150   |
| Steel Rail                     | 0.001 - 0.003   |
>[!tip] Observation
>Lower $\mu_{r}$ means
> -> less energy loss
> -> higher vehicle efficiency
> -> longer EV driving range

##### 4. Collect Vehicle Parameters
Example: Tata Nexon EV
- Vehicle Mass, $m=1510 \ kg$
- Rolling Resistance Coefficient, $\mu_{r}=0.012$
- Gravitational Acceleration, $g = 9.81 \ m / s^{2}$
- Road: Flat road ($\theta=0^\circ$)

##### 5. Substitute the Values

using the equation $F_{rr}=\mu_{r}\times m\times g$ 
and upon substitution 
$$
F_{rr}= 0.012\times_{1}510\times 9.81
$$
##### 6. Calculate Rolling Resistance Force
Calculate vehicle weight
$1510 \times 9.81=14813.1N$
Now,
$F_{rr}=0.012\times 14813.1$
$F_{rr}=177.7N$
Final Rolling Resistance Force
$\boxed{F_{rr}=177.7N}$

##### 7. Calculate Rolling Resistance Power
$$
P_{rr}=F_{rr}\times v
$$

| Symbol   | Meaning                  |
| -------- | ------------------------ |
| $P_{rr}$ | Rolling Resistance Power |
| $F_{rr}$ | Rolling Resistance Force |
| $v$      | Vehicle Speed            |
##### 8. Convert Vehicle Speed
Vehicle speed = 100 km/h
Convert to m/s
$$v = \dfrac{100\times 100}{3600}$$
$$
\boxed{v=27.78 \  m/s}
$$
##### 9. Calculate Rolling Resistance Power
Substitute
$$
P_{rr}=177.7 \times 27.78
$$
$$
P_{rr}=4937 W
$$
Convert to kilowatts

$$
\boxed{P_{rr} = 4.94 \ kW}
$$
##### 10. Interpretation
At 100 km/h,
Rolling resistance consumes $\boxed{4.94\ kW}$ of battery power continuously. This energy is permanently lost while driving.

##### 11. Compare with Aerodynamic Drag (at 100 km/h)

| Force              | Power Required |
| ------------------ | -------------- |
| Rolling Resistance | 4.94 kW        |
| Aerodynamic Drag   | 11.5 kW        |
**Observation:** at high speeds, aerodynamic drag requires more than twice the power needed to overcome rolling resistance.

##### 12. Effect of Speed on Rolling Resistance
- The rolling resistance force remains nearly constant because vehicle mass is constant and tyre coefficient changes very little with speed. 
- However, the power increases directly with speed because 
  $P_{rr}=F_{rr}\times v$.
- If speed doubles, rolling resistance power also doubles.
- Rolling resistance power is linearly proportional to vehicle speed.

##### 13. Rolling Resistance VS Aerodynamic Drag

| Vehicle Speed  | Dominant Resistance                           |
| -------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 0 - 40 km/h    | Rolling Resistance                            |
| 40 - 60 km/h   | Rolling Resistance $\approx$ Aerodynamic Drag |
| Above 100 km/h | Aerodynamic Drag Dominates                    |

>[! Key Insight]  
>- Below approximately 60 km/h, rolling resistance is generally greater than aerodynamic drag.
>- Above 100 km/h, aerodynamic drag becomes the dominant resistive force and consumes significantly more battery power.

##### 14. Factors Affecting $\mu_{r}$ (Rolling Resistance Coefficient)

| Factor            | Effect                                                    |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Low Tyre Pressure | 10% pressure drop -> 3 - 5% increase in $\mu_{r}$         |
| Cold Temperature  | Approximately 20% increase in $\mu_{r}$ during cold start |
| Tyre Compound     | Silica-based "green" tyres reduce $\mu_{r}$ by 15 - 25%   |
