# TCO Methodology and Economics

**Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) is a lifecycle financial analysis comparing the upfront and operational costs of an EV versus an internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicle.**

## The Three Main Cost Components

### 1. Acquisition Cost ($C_{acq}$)
- **Definition:** The purchase price, taxes, and registration.
- **EV Context:** Typically **20-50% higher** than ICE due to battery costs. Subsidies (like FAME-II) are designed to bridge this gap.

### 2. Energy/Fuel Cost ($C_{energy}$)
- **Definition:** Ongoing cost of fuel (Petrol/Diesel) or electricity.
- **EV Context:** Significantly lower. Typical running costs are **₹0.20–0.50/km** (EV) vs **₹2.00–4.00/km** (ICE).

### 3. Maintenance Cost ($C_{maint}$)
- **Definition:** Scheduled servicing, oil changes, and repairs.
- **EV Context:** ~30-50% lower. EVs have no oil filters, spark plugs, or complex transmissions. Regenerative braking also reduces brake pad wear.

## Why It Matters

Consumers often make decisions based on the "Sticker Price" (Acquisition). TCO analysis proves that for high-utilization users (fleets, taxis), the EV is actually the **cheaper** vehicle over a 5-year period despite the higher starting price.

> [!example] Indian 2W Comparison
> - **Petrol 2W:** Low upfront (₹80k), high running (₹2.5/km).
> - **EV 2W:** High upfront (₹1.2L), low running (₹0.3/km).
> The EV pays for its premium after ~25,000 km of riding.
