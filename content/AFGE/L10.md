# 17/08/26 Mon 14:00pm


- **Internal Combustion Engines**: **Brake Power** (BP) is the actual usable power available at the engine's crankshaft or output shaft.  It is calculated as the difference between the **Indicated Power** (power developed in the cylinder) and **Frictional Power** (power lost to internal friction).  It is measured using devices like dynamometers and is the standard metric for evaluating an engine's performance efficiency ($BP = IP - FP$).


**Volumetric Efficiency** in an **internal combustion engine** design refers to the efficiency with which the engine can move the _charge_ of fresh air into and out of the [cylinders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder_\(engine\) "Cylinder (engine)"). It also denotes the ratio of equivalent air volume drawn into the cylinder to the cylinder's swept volume.[5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volumetric_efficiency#cite_note-5) This equivalent volume is commonly inserted into a mass estimation equation based upon [Boyle's Gas Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyle's_law "Boyle's law"). When VE is multiplied by the cylinder volume, an accurate estimate of cylinder air mass (charge) can be made for use in determining the required fuel delivery and spark timing for the engine.
